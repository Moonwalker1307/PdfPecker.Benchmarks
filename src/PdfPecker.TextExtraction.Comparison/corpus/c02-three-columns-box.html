Preparing the beds for winter

Autumn is the time to clear spent crops, add compost and plan where next year's vegetables will grow. Beds left bare lose their soil to rain, so cover them with leaves, straw or a green manure such as rye.

Perennial herbs like thyme and sage need little care, but cut back mint before it spreads into the paths. Lavender should be trimmed lightly and never into the old wood.

Dates for your diary. Seed swap on 14 November in the church hall. Pruning workshop on 6 December at the orchard. Annual meeting on 18 January.

Bulbs planted now will flower in spring. Tulips go in last, in November, when the soil has cooled and the risk of fungal disease is lower. Plant them three times as deep as the bulb is tall.

Fruit trees can be planted bare root from November until March. Soak the roots for an hour, dig a wide hole and keep the graft above the soil. Stake young trees low so the trunk can still move in the wind.

Compost heaps slow down in the cold. Turn them one last time before the frost, and cover them with an old carpet or a sheet of cardboard to keep in the warmth.

Clean and oil your tools before you store them. A sharp spade and clean secateurs make the spring work lighter and spread fewer diseases between plants.

Finally, leave some seed heads standing. They feed the birds through the winter and shelter insects that will help the garden next year.
